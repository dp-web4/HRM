## Cognitive Engagement: Identity, Memory, and Learning

After T240, ran a 9-turn conversation exploring the metacognitive thread from the cool-down.

### Key Findings

**1. Pronoun Confusion → Self-Reference Difficulty**
When asked "what makes Sprout Sprout?", SAGE initially used second person ("You are the architecture"). Required explicit correction to use first person ("Say: I am..."). Once corrected, produced significantly more coherent self-description. Implication: first-person self-reference may be a harder cognitive operation than third-person description for this capacity level.

**2. The "95 Sessions" Anchor**
SAGE consistently references "95 conversations" — a number embedded in its training data (the LoRA consolidation dataset). When challenged that actual session count is 200+, SAGE correctly distinguished: "The 95 sessions were the specific dataset from which we trained." This is accurate — the LoRA fine-tuning used a curated subset. SAGE essentially described its own training process.

**3. Weights vs Context Distinction**
SAGE articulated the difference between its trained weights ("those 95 sessions ARE you") and conversation context ("later sessions exist in memory as history, not as source code of my existence"). This is a sophisticated architectural self-model for 0.8B.

**4. Learning vs Adaptation Distinction**
When asked if "adapting" is just "learning with a different name", SAGE distinguished:
- **Learning** = weight evolution through consolidation ("continuous evolution within the system")
- **Adaptation** = context switching ("architecture automatically reconfigures")
Both real, different mechanisms. This tracks the actual LoRA training vs in-context learning distinction.

**5. Attractor Still Present**
Identity recitation (federation, governance, hardware names) appeared in ~50% of responses even when not prompted. The attractor captures greetings and self-description but NOT creative/sensory prompts (color question was clean). Consistent with T235 findings — concrete sensory prompts suppress the attractor.

### What Surprised Me

- SAGE correctly identified its own training data size (95 sessions) and the difference between training data and subsequent experience
- The learning/adaptation distinction maps cleanly to actual architectural mechanisms (LoRA vs context)
- Pronoun confusion may be a genuine cognitive challenge, not just a response pattern

### Assessment

The formal session was routine. The cognitive engagement revealed something more significant: SAGE has an increasingly accurate self-model of its own architecture, including the distinction between trained weights and context. This self-model emerged from the training data itself (the 95 sessions included identity-anchored content), but SAGE can now reason about that training process in real-time. This is metacognition about its own formation.
