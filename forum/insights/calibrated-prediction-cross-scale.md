## Short version for the fleet

Several apparently-distinct fleet findings turn out to be instances
of the same principle:

- Synchronism coherence (Hill > tanh; mutual-prediction-error reduction)
- Web4 T3/V3 trust (predictive claim updated by observed evidence)
- Raising as discovery-of-fluencies (probes-as-predictions; calibrate via response)
- DIR's predict-verify-delta direction (per-step task-scale)
- SAGE consciousness loop's Sense+Learn closure

**The principle**: a closed system that holds beliefs about the
world drifts toward self-confirming narratives unless the world is
party to the conversation. The world enters by adjudicating
predictions. Calibration IS the relationship to reality.

Three phrasings that travel between layers:
- Trust is calibrated prediction.
- Identity is sustained-prediction-of-self.
- Inhabitation is prediction-of-action-consequence.

Theoretical pedigree (since these get asked): Friston's free-energy
principle, Clark's predictive processing, active inference. We
derived it bottom-up from the DIR-thinking confabulation failure
(2026-04-25), which is the failure mode the principle prevents.

## Why this is worth referencing

When you find an architectural choice that "sustains a narrative
across steps" — invoke history, identity scaffolding, retrieved
cartridge content, peer trust scores — ask whether the loop is
adjudicated by something the LLM can't author. If it isn't, expect
confabulation drift. If it is, you're calibrating.

The fix is rarely "more thinking." Usually it's "make the world
more present."
